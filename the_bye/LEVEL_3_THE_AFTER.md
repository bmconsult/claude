## The After: Optimal Systems by Pillar

### Overarching Structure: Federated Bioregionalism

Not one world government (top-down control).
Not isolated silos (no coordination).
**Federated bioregions** (local autonomy + global connection).

**The Model (Sourced):**

Based on [Elinor Ostrom's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom) Nobel Prize-winning research on commons governance and [bioregionalism](https://earth.org/bioregionalism/):

| Level | What It Handles | How It's Governed |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Local** (village/neighborhood) | Daily life, immediate needs | Direct democracy, consensus |
| **Community** (town/city) | Services, infrastructure | Sociocracy, elected circles |
| **Bioregion** (watershed/ecosystem) | Resources, ecology | Federated councils |
| **Continental** | Trade, movement, large-scale coordination | Representative federation |
| **Global** | Planetary commons (atmosphere, oceans, space) | Minimal global council |

**Key Principle: Subsidiarity**

From [world federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federalism): Decisions made at the lowest level possible. Global only handles what local cannot.

> "In a world federation based on subsidiarity, the delegation is the other way round, from local to central. Global federal government is subsidiary to local in that it only does what local government cannot."

### 1. Economy: Circulation

**Current:** Extraction pyramid (wealth flows up)
**Optimal:** Circulation network (wealth flows around)

**Sourced Principles:**

From [Ostrom's 8 Principles](https://www.agrariantrust.org/ostroms-eight-design-principles-for-a-successfully-managed-commons/) for commons management:

1. **Clearly defined boundaries** - Who's in, who's out
2. **Rules adapted to local context** - No one-size-fits-all
3. **Participatory decision-making** - Users make the rules
4. **Monitoring** - Community-accountable oversight
5. **Graduated sanctions** - Warnings before bans
6. **Conflict resolution** - Built-in mechanisms
7. **Recognition by external authorities** - Legal standing
8. **Nested enterprises** - Local embedded in regional

**Mechanisms (Sourced + Novel):**

| Mechanism | Source | How It Works |
|-----------|--------|--------------|
| **Commons ownership** | Ostrom (1990) | Land, water, essential resources held collectively |
| **Mutual credit** | [LETS systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_exchange_trading_system) (1983) | Internal currency, balances to zero |
| **Timebanking** | [Edgar Cahn](https://timebanks.org/) (1980s) | Hour = hour, regardless of skill |
| **Gift economy** | Indigenous traditions | Give what you can, take what you need |
| **Stewardship not ownership** | Novel synthesis | Land held by community, stewarded by individuals |

**No:**
- Interest (usury)
- Rent-seeking
- Private ownership of land
- Accumulation beyond need
- Absentee ownership

### 2. Political: Consent-Based

**Current:** Representative theater + corporate capture
**Optimal:** Multi-scale consent governance

**Sourced Model: Sociocracy**

From [Gerard Endenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocracy) (1970s), based on Quaker practices:

| Principle | What It Means |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Consent** | Decision stands if no one has reasoned objection |
| **Circles** | Semi-autonomous groups with defined domains |
| **Double-linking** | Two representatives between levels (up and down) |
| **Elections by consent** | Roles filled by nomination + consent |

**Multi-scale application:**

| Scale | Governance Method |
|-------|-------------------|
| Household | Consensus |
| Neighborhood (15-50) | Sociocracy |
| Community (50-500) | Federated sociocracy |
| Bioregion (500-50,000) | Representative + sortition |
| Continental/Global | Minimal council + sortition |

**Novel addition: Sortition**

From [ancient Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortition): Random selection for some roles, like jury duty. Prevents capture by career politicians.

## The Big Questions Answered

### Will There Be Internet?

**Maybe.** Plan for it not existing.

| Scenario | Communication |
|----------|---------------|
| Internet survives | Use it, don't depend on it |
| Internet fragments | Regional networks |
| Internet collapses | Mesh, radio, physical |

**Resilience principle:** Every critical system has non-digital backup.

### One World Government?

**Yes and no.**

- **Yes:** Global coordination for planetary commons (climate, oceans, space, pandemics)
- **No:** Not top-down control of daily life

**Model:** [Subsidiarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiarity) - decisions at lowest level possible. Global council handles only what bioregions cannot.

### Silos or Connected?

**Connected, but self-sufficient.**

Each bioregion can function autonomously if cut off. But trade, knowledge, and culture flow between them.

Like a body: organs can function briefly alone, but work better connected.

### How Do We Build It?

**Parallel construction.**

1. Build functioning examples now (The Bye communities)
2. Prove they work better
3. Absorb people as current system fails
4. Connect examples into network
5. Network becomes new default

Not revolution (violent overthrow). Not reform (working within broken system). **Exodus** - leave and build the alternative.

### How Do We Rebuild After Collapse?

**From the communities that survive.**

| What Survives | Why |
|---------------|-----|
| Skills | In people's heads |
| Relationships | Trust built |
| Seed knowledge | Books, memory, practice |
| Physical assets | Land, tools, buildings |
| Culture | Stories, rituals, values |

The Bye is the seed bank. When the old system fails, these communities become the templates for what replaces it.

### The Genesis Question

You asked: "Do they start over at Genesis?"

**No.** Not starting from nothing. Starting from:

- Accumulated human knowledge (preserve it)
- Functioning communities (The Bye)
- Proven models (Ostrom, sociocracy, etc.)
- Skills in living people
- Land and tools already acquired
- Relationships already built

Genesis was creation from void. This is transformation from broken to functional.

More like **Acts 2** than Genesis 1:

> "All the believers were together and had everything in common. They sold property and possessions to give to anyone who had need."

The early church was a liberation cooperative. Before the hierarchy, before Constantine, before the institution—just people sharing everything and taking care of each other.

That's the after. Not a return to the garden. A return to the community.
