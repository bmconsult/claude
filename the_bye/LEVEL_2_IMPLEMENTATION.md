## The Four Phases

### Phase 1: The Meantime (Now → Break)

**What it is:** Building within the system while it still functions.

**Timeline:** Unknown. Could be 2 years, 20 years, or gradual decay.

**What we're doing:**
- Running boot camps (training people)
- Building the network (community formation)
- Acquiring assets (land, tools, resources)
- Establishing governance (practicing sociocracy)
- Developing curriculum (what we teach)
- Creating infrastructure (platforms, communication)

**Key challenge:** Build fast enough to be ready, sustainable enough if the break takes decades.

**Metrics:**
- Number of people through boot camp
- Number who've reached severance
- Collective assets acquired
- Geographic distribution (nodes + sites)
- Teaching network depth

### Phase 2: The Break (Transition)

**What it is:** The system destabilizes. Could be sudden or gradual.

**Scenarios:**

| Scenario | Timeline | Characteristics |
|----------|----------|-----------------|
| Sudden collapse | Days-months | Financial failure, supply chains break, chaos |
| Gradual decay | Years-decades | Slow erosion, increasing dysfunction |
| Managed transition | Decades | Policy shifts, UBI, system transforms |
| Fragmentation | Varies | Some regions collapse, others don't |

**What we need ready:**
- Physical locations (shelter, food, water)
- Communication systems (if internet fails)
- Supply chains (food, medicine, essentials)
- Security protocols (if things get rough)
- Absorption capacity (for refugees from system)
- Community cohesion (trust already built)

**Key challenge:** Be robust to multiple scenarios.

### Phase 3: Emergence (Post-Break)

**What it is:** Old system gone or irrelevant. New order forming.

**What we're doing:**
- Providing stability for members
- Potentially absorbing new people
- Establishing governance at scale
- Securing resources
- Building the new economy in practice
- Interfacing with whatever emerges externally

**Key challenge:** Scale during crisis without losing cohesion.

### Phase 4: The After (Timeless)

**What it is:** The destination. Functioning society. Not utopia - reality.

**What it looks like:**
- Self-sufficient or sustainably-trading communities
- Governance that works (sociocracy, consent-based)
- Economy that's fair (circulation, not extraction)
- Ecology that's regenerative
- Culture that's alive
- Everyone has enough
- No extraction, no prisoners

**The ending:**
- Formal Bye structure dissolves
- Community continues informally
- Relationships persist, institution ends
- Nothing left to escape from

## Governance: Sociocracy

Based on research, [sociocracy](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/) is recommended for The Bye.

### Why Sociocracy

- Designed for communities (not businesses like Holacracy)
- Lower cost to implement
- Flexible, customizable
- Based on consent (no objections) not consensus (full agreement)
- Circles handle different domains
- Scales through federation

### Core Principles

1. **Consent decision-making:** A decision is made when no one has a reasoned objection
2. **Circles:** Semi-autonomous groups with defined domains
3. **Double-linking:** Each circle sends two representatives to the next level (leader + elected delegate)
4. **Elections by consent:** Roles filled by nomination and consent

### Circle Structure for The Bye

```
                    ┌─────────────┐
                    │   General   │
                    │   Circle    │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │
        ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
        │                  │                  │
  ┌─────┴─────┐     ┌─────┴─────┐     ┌─────┴─────┐
  │ Training  │     │  Assets   │     │ Community │
  │  Circle   │     │  Circle   │     │  Circle   │
  └───────────┘     └───────────┘     └───────────┘
        │                  │                  │
   Boot camps         Land, tools        Membership
```

## Economy: Circulation Not Extraction

### The Sorting Hat (Matching Contributions)

| Who Enters | What They Bring | What They Need |
|------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Rich wanting out | Cash, resources, assets | Skills, consciousness, community |
| Poor with hustle | Skills, time, energy | Resources, support, runway |
| Skilled but trapped | Teaching ability, expertise | Freedom, community |
| Connected | Network, access | Skills, direction |
| Young | Time, energy, adaptability | Knowledge, mentorship |
| Experienced | Wisdom, pattern recognition | Energy, hope |

**Principle:** Everyone brings equal value. Different forms, same worth.

### Internal Economy Options

| System | How It Works | Best For |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| **Timebanking** | 1 hour = 1 hour, regardless of skill | Services, equalizing |
| **Mutual credit** | Internal currency, balances to zero | Trading, tracking |
| **Gift economy** | Give what you can, take what you need | High-trust, small scale |
| **Commons** | Shared resources with use rules | Land, tools, infrastructure |
| **Hybrid** | Different systems for different things | Reality |

**Recommendation:** Start with gift economy + commons for core needs, add timebanking for services, mutual credit for larger exchanges.

### Resource Flows

```
IN:
├── Member contributions (cash, skills, time, assets)
├── Collective assets generating value
├── External work by members (optional)
└── New member contributions

OUT:
├── Member needs (training, support, resources)
├── Collective infrastructure
├── Reserve for emergencies
└── New member support
```

## The Civilization Pillars

What needs to work for a functioning society:

### 1. Economy
- Mutual credit + timebanking + gift hybrid
- Collective ownership of major assets
- Real production (food, goods, services)
- Trade with outside when useful

### 2. Political System
- Sociocracy (consent-based circles)
- Elected roles, rotating leadership
- Written constitution
- Conflict resolution protocols

### 3. Education
- Integrated with community life
- Apprenticeship model
- Boot camp curriculum for new members
- Ongoing learning circles

### 4. Governance
- Self-governing circles
- Minimal interface with external government (compliance where required)
- Internal agreements binding
- Transparent decision-making

### 5. Medical
- Preventive focus (nutrition, exercise, community health)
- Trained members for common issues
- Integration with external healthcare for serious needs
- Collective health fund

### 6. Food
- Community agriculture (minimum 50% self-sufficient target)
- Food preservation, storage
- Shared meals (community building)
- Trade/purchase for what we don't produce

### 7. Spirituality
- Shared practices (doesn't have to be religious)
- Rituals for transitions (entry, severance, graduation)
- Space for individual practice
- Community meaning-making

### 8. Legal
- Written internal agreements
- External compliance where required
- Conflict resolution before external courts
- Asset protection structures

### 9. Sciences
- Practical application (agriculture, health, building)
- Not rejection of knowledge
- Research for community benefit
- Documentation and learning

### 10. Civil
- Shared spaces maintained
- Infrastructure collectively built
- Roles for upkeep
- Beauty and functionality

## Location Strategy

### Criteria

| Factor | Priority | Considerations |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Water | Critical | Rainfall, aquifer, river access |
| Climate | High | Survivable without grid, growing season |
| Agriculture | High | Soil quality, growing days |
| Natural disasters | High | Avoid hurricanes, earthquakes, flood zones |
| Political climate | Medium | Friendly to alternative communities |
| Cost | Medium | Land prices, taxes, development costs |
| Access | Medium | Distance from cities, road access |
| Existing community | Medium | Neighbors, culture fit |

| Region | Pros | Cons |

| **Northern New England** | Community, water, seasons | Cold, rocky soil, expensive coast |
